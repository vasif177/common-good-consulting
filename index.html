The Nonprofit Challenge

Your mission is too important for these risks

Most nonprofits don't fail because of bad intentions — they fail because operational and compliance gaps go unaddressed until they become crises.

📋

Compliance Exposure

Unregistered solicitations, late 990 filings, or unfiled state reports can cost your organization its exempt status — and its donor trust.

💸

Grant Risk

Federal grants require rigorous cost documentation. Without proper time-tracking, cost allocation, and procurement controls, findings and disallowed costs are inevitable.

🏛️

Board Governance Gaps

Boards that lack written conflict-of-interest policies, audit committees, or documented compensation practices face IRS scrutiny and reputational risk.

📊

Weak Financial Controls

Commingled funds, unreconciled accounts, and absent internal controls aren't just accounting problems — they're red flags to auditors, funders, and regulators.

👥

Staff & Succession Risk

When institutional knowledge lives in one person's head and there's no succession plan, leadership transitions become existential threats.

📉

Unmeasured Impact

Funders increasingly require outcome data — not just outputs. Without logic models and evaluation frameworks, you can't demonstrate what your programs actually achieve.

Compliance Landscape

The rules nonprofits must know

Nonprofits operate under a layered web of federal, state, and funder requirements. Staying compliant isn't optional — regulators, watchdog organizations, and donors are watching.

We help you understand every layer of this landscape and build systems to stay ahead of it — not scramble to catch up.

Key compliance thresholds

< $50K gross receipts Form 990-N (e-Postcard)
$50K–$199K Form 990-EZ
≥ $200K receipts or ≥$500K assets Full Form 990 (public record)
≥ $750K federal expenditure Single Audit (2 CFR Part 200)

Federal / IRS

IRS & 501(c)(3) Rules

Your tax-exempt status comes with ongoing obligations that must be met every year — not just at formation.

  • Annual Form 990 filing (990-N, 990-EZ, or full 990)
  • Private benefit & inurement prohibitions — absolute for insiders
  • Lobbying limits — 501(h) election creates a safe harbor
  • Absolute ban on political campaign activity
  • Reasonable compensation with documented comparability data
  • Unrelated Business Income Tax (Form 990-T if UBTI >$1,000)
  • Donor acknowledgment for gifts ≥$250 and quid pro quo >$75
  • Foreign activities reporting via Schedule F
Single Audit (Federal Grants)

2 CFR Part 200 — Uniform Guidance

Any organization expending ≥$750K in federal awards in a fiscal year must undergo a Single Audit — combining a financial audit with a federal program compliance audit.

  • Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (SEFA)
  • Allowable cost principles — direct, indirect, allocable, necessary
  • Established indirect cost rate or de minimis 10% of MTDC
  • Procurement standards (micro-purchase, competitive, sole-source)
  • Personnel cost documentation — time & effort reporting
  • Subrecipient monitoring obligations
  • Cash management — minimize drawdown-to-disbursement float
  • Program income tracking and reporting
State & Multi-State

State Charitable Registration

41 states plus DC require registration before soliciting contributions — including online solicitations that reach residents of other states.

  • Annual renewal in each state where soliciting
  • Unified Registration Statement (URS) accepted by some states
  • State-specific audited financial statement thresholds
  • Annual corporate report to Secretary of State
  • Attorney General notifications for major transactions
  • CA: RRF-1 / CT-TR-1 to Registry of Charitable Trusts
  • NY: CHAR500; audit required ≥$250K revenue
  • Lapsed registration = illegal solicitation; immediate risk
Board Governance

IRS-Recommended Governance Policies

Form 990 Part VI asks whether your board has these policies. Absence invites scrutiny — and real liability.

  • Written conflict of interest policy + annual disclosures
  • Whistleblower protection policy (document destruction is criminal)
  • Document retention & destruction policy
  • Executive compensation: rebuttable presumption procedure
  • Audit committee (independent of management)
  • Independent board majority — no material financial relationships
  • Board chair and CEO separate positions (best practice)
  • Term limits to prevent entrenchment
Financial Standards

FASB ASC 958 — Nonprofit Accounting

Nonprofit financial statements follow unique GAAP standards. Misapplication can materially misstate your financial position to funders and regulators.

  • Net assets: with vs. without donor restrictions
  • Statement of Functional Expenses required — program/G&A/fundraising
  • Liquidity & availability disclosure (ASU 2016-14)
  • Contributions vs. exchange transactions — different recognition rules
  • Conditional vs. unconditional contribution recognition
  • In-kind contributions valued at fair market value
  • Endowment fund accounting & underwater fund disclosure
  • Restricted fund release requires documented purpose fulfillment
Grant Compliance

Federal & Private Grant Requirements

Grant funds are almost always restricted. Without proper infrastructure, grants become liabilities — not assets.

  • Grant-by-grant tracking in accounting system
  • Personnel: semi-annual certifications or monthly effort reports
  • Documented, consistently applied cost allocation methodology
  • Subrecipient agreements and monitoring required for pass-throughs
  • Matching / in-kind: documented and correctly valued
  • Grant close-out: final reports, unspent fund return
  • Record retention: 3 years post-final federal report (often 7 years per agreement)
  • SAM.gov registration current for federal awards
